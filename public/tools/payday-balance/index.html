    How this payday balance calculator works

    This tool helps you answer one practical question before payday: out of the balance currently sitting in your checking account, how much could you move to savings or another goal without shorting upcoming bills, everyday spending, or your safety buffer?

    Worked fictional example

    Suppose your current checking balance is $2,200.00, your next expected payday is in 4 days, you have an unpaid $315.00 utility bill due today and a $900.00 rent payment due in 2 days, you plan $0.00 in other spending, and you want to leave a $200.00 buffer untouched.

    The calculator subtracts both unpaid bills ($315.00 + $900.00 = $1,215.00), other spending ($0.00), and your buffer ($200.00) from $2,200.00, leaving an estimated $785.00 you could move. If you also want to explore a fixed interactive walkthrough, see the fictional cash-timing demo.

    Pending vs. already-deducted bills, overdue bills, and payday bills

    • Pending vs. already deducted: If a bill or card charge has already reduced the checking balance you entered, mark it as already deducted so it is not subtracted twice.
    • Overdue bills: Any unpaid bill dated before today is still included until you pay or deduct it.
    • Bills due on payday: Bills due on your payday itself are conservatively included, because an automatic debit may clear before your paycheck arrives.
    • Bills after payday: Bills due after your next expected payday are excluded from this short-horizon calculation and shown separately.

    Planned daily spending vs. buffer

    Other spending until payday covers expected day-to-day costs like groceries, fuel, or transit that are not entered as separate bill rows. The amount to leave untouched is an extra cushion you choose to keep in the account. If the total exceeds your balance, the calculator shows $0.00 transferable and reports the shortfall against your plan separately.
